Cornerstone · ecommerce content automation

from product to instagram post

Turning a product catalog and a creative brief into an image, a caption, and a reviewable Instagram draft.

my contribution
Rails workflow, AI generation, and publishing integration
built with
Ruby on Rails, Spree, OpenAI, Active Job, Active Storage, Meta Graph API

out in the world

Three actual posts from Cornerstone Team Sports’ Instagram account. Each screenshot shows the published image alongside its caption. Open any screenshot for a closer look, or follow the link to the original post.

the challenge

A product already has a name, an image, and a place in the store’s catalog. Turning it into social content still means deciding what to say, creating a visual, checking the details, and getting the post onto Instagram.

I wanted to bring those steps into Cornerstone’s existing ecommerce admin, so the team could work from the products they already manage and keep control over what goes live.

one brief, a reviewable draft

The workflow starts with a few concrete choices: the store, campaign goal, visual mood, composition, and products to feature.

  1. BriefChoose products and creative direction.
  2. GenerateCreate an image, caption, and hashtags.
  3. ReviewEdit the copy or regenerate either part.
  4. PublishSend the reviewed draft to Instagram.

Generation runs in a background job and saves the result as a draft. The image and caption stay together in the admin, with an explicit publish action after review.

inside the admin

The preseason fueling post above, as it appears in Spree’s Content Creation screen. The image, caption, creative brief, publication status, prompt, and model details are kept together in one record.

the decisions behind it

give the model product context

The prompt combines brand voice, audience, seasonal context, campaign goal, and product details. Catalog images serve as visual references, with instructions to preserve packaging, labels, colors, and proportions while composing a new scene. A separate logo overlay adds store branding after generation.

Those references guide the result; they do not guarantee accurate packaging or text. Human review remains part of the workflow.

let the team keep what works

A good image should not need to be discarded because the caption needs another pass. Image and caption regeneration are separate actions, and the team can edit the caption and hashtags or replace the image before publishing.

treat publishing as its own step

The publishing service checks that the draft has an image and a caption, provides Meta with a publicly reachable image URL, creates a media container, and requests publication. If Meta is still processing the image, it retries that publication request a bounded number of times.

Successful publication saves the Instagram media ID and timestamp. Failures are recorded on the draft so the admin can see what went wrong.

keep the work traceable

The draft retains its products, assembled prompt, generation settings, model information, and timing metadata. Separate services handle creation, regeneration, and publication, making each part easier to inspect and test.

what this makes possible

The team can move from catalog products to a generated visual and post description, refine the draft, and publish to Instagram from the same admin. The repetitive content preparation is automated while the final editorial decision stays with a person.

The implementation includes specs for draft creation, independent image and caption regeneration, publishing validation, and Meta API error handling. I’m describing the delivered workflow here; I haven’t measured time savings or engagement lift.

My takeaway: the useful part of this AI feature is the whole path around generation—real product context, a draft someone can change, and a clear handoff to publishing.
